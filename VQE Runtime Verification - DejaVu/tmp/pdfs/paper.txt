I. INTRODUCTION
•Motivation.VQE is a widely-used near-term hybrid
algorithm for ground-state estimation; correctness de-
pends on both the quantum subroutine and the classical
optimization loop interacting under noise and finite shot
budgets.
•The verification gap.Current efforts are empirical
(benchmarks, ablations) or singular formal models on
algorithm correctness or mathematical proof. No unified
framework spans logical safety, numeric invariants, prob-
abilistic reliability, and runtime trace conformance in a
formal verification way.
•Motivating example.A single natural-language
property—“no optimizer update before energy
aggregation”—admits four distinct formal encodings
(CTL in NuXmv, an SMT obligation, a PRISM
probability bound, and a DejaVu past-time monitor).
Each catches a different class of failure. Use this as the
running example throughout the paper.
•Research questions.
–RQ1: Can VQE properties be systematically orga-
nized into a layered architecture grounded in one
shared execution model?
–RQ2: For each layer, which property classes are
tractable, and which are infeasible?
–RQ3: When do guarantees compose across layers?
–RQ4: What is the verification cost and bug-class
coverage of each layer?
•Paper organization.The rest of the paper is organized as
follows. Section II provides the necessary background on
the variational quantum eigensolver and the four formal
methods underlying our architecture. Section III defines
the shared VQE execution model. Section IV introduces
our property taxonomy and contract library, organizing
the VQE property space into eleven categories. Section V
presents the four-layer verification architecture, detailing
the encoding, coverage, and expressiveness limits of
each layer. Section VI establishes cross-layer guaran-
tees, including refinement relationships among encodings
and assume-guarantee composition results. Section VII
demonstrates the architecture on representative VQE in-
stances. Section VIII evaluates the framework against our
research questions through mutation testing and scalabil-
ity analysis. Section IX introduces the related work on
the verification of the hybrid quantum-classical systems.
Section X discusses limitations and scope. Section XI
concludes the paper.

Overall, the NuXmv formal verification establishes that
the VQE hybrid execution loop satisfies the logical protocol
required by the shared execution model: parameters are bound
before measurement, all required measurement terms are com-
pleted before aggregation, optimizer updates are performed
only after valid energy computation, iteration progress is well
structured, and execution terminates in valid conditions. These
guarantees form the logical foundation of the verification
framework. The subsequent layers strengthen this foundation
by checking numerical validity, probabilistic reliability under
finite-shot uncertainty, and runtime conformance of observed
executions to the intended semantics.
Summary:The goal of the logical verification layer is not
to find defects in a deliberately correct canonical VQE shared
execution model. Rather, it is to formalize the VQE control
loop as a machine-checkable reference semantics. The NuXmv
model specifies the legal ordering of preparation, measure-
ment, aggregation, convergence checking, optimization, and
termination. Verifying the model establishes that the reference
execution protocol is internally coherent: optimizer updates
cannot occur before energy aggregation, terminal states are
reached only through valid convergence or error conditions,
and non-terminal modes preserve progress. These results pro-
vide a logical correctness baseline for the remaining layers.

C. Probabilistic Verification (Storm)
The probabilistic verification layer analyzes the effects
of finite-shot measurement uncertainty on VQE convergence
decisions, the bounded convergence probability, the expected
iteration count, and the expected shot consumption. Due to
measurement stochasticity, at each iteration, the VQE process
can take a different path with probability because of the
energy evaluation and parameter update. However, an exact
Discrete-Time Markov Chain (DTMC) for formal verifica-
tion requires enumeration of the possible finite-shot count
outcomes for the measured Pauli groups and calculation of
their induced energy-estimator probabilities. The number of
combinations grows rapidly with the shot count and number
of measurement groups, making direct construction imprac-
tical at meaningful shot budgets. We therefore approximate
each candidate’s finite-shot energy estimator using a Gaussian
distribution parameterized by its analytically derived mean and
variance. Storm exhaustively analyzes all paths of the Gaussian
DTMC, rather than all measurement outcomes of the original

These results indicate an optimistic analytical–empirical dis-

checks all the possible paths with Gaussian approximation,
whereas every empirical run follows the original finite-shot
sampling semantics. In addition, the empirical quantities are
estimated from a finite number of executions with measure-

The current model isolates finite-shot measurement un-
certainty. It does not yet include gate noise, readout error,
backend failure, hardware drift, crosstalk, leakage, or other
device-specific effects. Although Gaussian abstraction avoids
direct enumeration of high-shot measurement outcomes, the
reachable state space can still grow with the iteration bound,
the number of optimizer candidates, and the number of distinct
optimizer states.
Overall, the Gaussian DTMC verified with Storm provides a
practical probabilistic verification approach for analyzing VQE
convergence reliability and resource consumption under finite-
shot uncertainty. By exhaustively evaluating probabilistic and
reward properties over the analytical abstraction, the model
provides quantitative information that can guide the choice of
iteration and shot budgets before costly VQE executions.
Summary:The probabilistic verification layer constructs a
tractable Gaussian DTMC from the finite-shot energy statistics
of VQE candidate circuits and uses Storm to exhaustively
evaluate bounded-convergence and resource properties over
this abstraction. Repeated finite-shot executions provide an
independent empirical reference for comparison. For the eval-
uatedH 2 configuration, both approaches exhibit the same
broad convergence progression, while the Gaussian DTMC
predicts systematically earlier and more likely convergence.
The observed analytical–empirical discrepancy is reported
explicitly to characterize the approximation behavior of the
Gaussian abstraction under finite empirical estimation.

X. DISCUSSION ANDLIMITATIONS
•Hardware noise modeling.Current abstraction assumes
a declared shot-noise model; richer hardware-specific
noise (crosstalk, leakage, drift) is future work.
•Concurrent and distributed VQE.The shared model
is sequential; extending to multi-backend or batched
execution is open.
•Floating-point semantics in the classical optimizer.Not
currently modeled; bounded by numeric-layer invariants
but not formally verified.
•Adversarial backends.The architecture assumes a co-
operative cloud backend; security verification is out of
scope.
•Generalization to other hybrid algorithms.QAOA,
VQD, and quantum machine learning loops fit the same
skeleton; sketch the migration path.
